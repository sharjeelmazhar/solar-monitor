⚡ Wiring the new ESP32-S3 board

For Yasir · Solar Monitor · board YD-ESP32-23 (ESP32-S3 N16R8) · 9 Oct 2026

✅ Our inverters (Inverex Veyron, Galaxy): 3 wires is everything Their RJ45 jack is an RS232 port. RS232 has only 3 lines: send, receive, ground. The inverter sends all its data (solar, PV1/PV2, battery, grid, load, settings, faults) over those 3. Extra wires would carry nothing more.
➕ Neighbours / other brands: add one small RS485 module now Nitrox, Growatt, SolaX (Fronus), Solis, Sungrow, Huawei use a different "language" called RS485 Modbus, on different RJ45 pins, and every brand puts them on different pins. So we add a second module and a screw-terminal RJ45 jack, so any brand can be wired by moving 2–3 screw wires, without re-crimping.

Cable side: A, B, GND
  • A (+) and B (−) go to the two RS485 pins of that brand's port. GND to its ground pin, if it has one.
  • Every brand is different (for example SolaX uses pins 4 and 5). We look it up in the brand's manual before connecting.
  • If the readings are garbage, swap A and B. That is safe.
  • Two boards share 3V3 and GND: use the board's two GND pins, or join the wires.

4 Why not just connect more wires to the same cable?

  • More wires ≠ more data. The data comes from the language the inverter speaks. Voltronic (ours) speaks RS232 on pins 1, 2, 8 and sends everything there.
  • Other brands speak RS485, on other pins, and sometimes on a different port (labelled RS485, COM, BMS, Meter or DRM). The RS485 module covers them.
  • The screw-terminal RJ45 breakout lets us move wires per brand in 1 minute instead of cutting cables.
  • Growatt SPF also has a USB port: that goes into the S3's USB socket later, no wires needed.

5 Safety rules

  • Never plug our cable into a neighbour's inverter before we check its port. Send a photo of the label (brand + model) and the ports first.
  • Never connect RS232 and RS485 to the same RJ45 pins at the same time. Only one module is wired to the cable per inverter.
  • Never put the inverter cable straight onto the ESP pins (RS232 is ±12 V). Always through a module.
  • Modules run on 3V3, never 5 V. Don't use pins 0, 3, 19, 20, 35, 36, 37, 43, 44, 45, 46, 48.
  • Power the board from one USB cable only (COM port).
